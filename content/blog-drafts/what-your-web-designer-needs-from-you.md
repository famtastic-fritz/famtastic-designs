The slow part of building a website is almost never the building. It's waiting.

Waiting on a logo file. Waiting on someone to decide whether it's "Emergency Callouts" or "24/7 Service." Waiting on photos that are still on a phone. We can build a site in two days. Projects that take two months take two months because of the gaps between emails.

Here's everything we need, so you can get it together in one sitting.

## 1. Your business details, exactly as you want them public

Sounds obvious, and it's the most common source of delay because there are more decisions here than people expect:

- The business name as it should appear — including whether "LLC" is on the site or not
- The phone number customers should call, if it's different from your personal one
- The email address that should receive enquiries
- Your address, or your service area if you travel to customers
- Your hours, including what happens on weekends and holidays

Write these out once. They'll be reused on every page and in your search listings, and they need to match everywhere.

## 2. Your services, in your customers' words

List what you do, with a sentence or two each.

The most useful thing you can do here is write them the way a customer would say them, not the way the trade does. Someone searching doesn't type "hydro-jetting services." They type "drain keeps backing up."

If you have services people always ask about that you *don't* offer, say so. Ruling things out prevents wasted calls, and it's one of the more valuable things a site does for you.

## 3. Your logo and colors — or a clear "I don't have one"

Send the best logo file you have. A vector file (`.svg`, `.ai`, `.eps`) is ideal. A large PNG works. A screenshot of your logo taken from your own Facebook page does not, and we'd rather tell you that now than at the end.

If you don't have a logo, that's genuinely fine and very common. Say so up front, and we'll set your business name in type properly. That's included, and it looks deliberate — far better than a stretched image.

## 4. Photos, if you have real ones

Photos of your actual work, premises, or team beat stock photography every time. Customers can tell the difference, and it's one of the clearest trust signals on a small business site.

Practical guidance:

- Phone photos are fine. Modern phones are more than good enough.
- Shoot in daylight where you can.
- Send originals, not versions that have been through messaging apps — those get compressed to mush.
- Ten good photos beat fifty mediocre ones.

If you don't have any, we'll design around it. Say so rather than sending filler.

## 5. Anything you're required to say

Some businesses have to display licence numbers, insurance details, certifications, or specific disclaimers. Some have claims they're not allowed to make. If any of that applies to you, tell us at the start — it affects how pages are written, and it's expensive to retrofit.

## What we don't need

**A design.** You don't have to know what you want it to look like. Telling us which of your competitors' sites you like, and which you hate, is more useful than a brief.

**Finished copy.** Rough notes are fine. Bullet points are fine. A voice memo is fine. We'd rather have your actual words messily than polished text that doesn't sound like you.

**All of it at once.** Send what you have. We'll tell you what's missing rather than sitting on it silently.
