# 55 Cents a Day Series Editorial Audit

## Why this correction was required

The first published version inherited generic demand-library language about
Drupal, portals, automation, and systems that are not included in the $199 Web
Basics delivery contract. Its inline visuals were technology concepts rather
than campaign storytelling graphics. This was a scope and editorial failure,
not merely a preference issue.

## Corrections

- Rewrote all eight campaign articles around the actual Web Basics scope.
- Removed Drupal, React, AI-optimized, 48-hour, portal, ecommerce, and custom-
  application implications from the included $199 deliverable.
- Added recognizable objections: price, perceived lack of need, satisfaction
  with current business, referral dependence, social-media dependence, lack of
  time, and fear of a large technical project.
- Added concrete bakery, barber, consultant, restaurant, campaign, domain,
  hosting, intake, and growth examples.
- Distinguished an informational one-page website from ecommerce, a paid-
  campaign landing system, a multi-page website, and a custom system.
- Replaced generic technology images with custom campaign graphics featuring
  characters, objections, the trust gap, and the 55-cent value equation.
- Kept the exact price and renewal boundaries: one-time $199 purchase, included
  first-year basic hosting, $9.99/month hosting after the included year under
  disclosed terms, and separate annual domain renewal.

## Claim verification

| Claim | Source | Verification | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 74% of consumers use two or more websites when checking local-business reviews | BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025 | Verified in original report | Representative SurveyMonkey panel of 1,026 U.S. adults; self-reported behavior |
| More than three-quarters use video while researching local businesses | BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025 | Verified in original report | Does not measure website causality or guaranteed purchasing outcomes |
| 92% preferred business information from a website rather than a social page | Verisign 2015 U.S. Online Survey | Verified in source report | Historical survey; 787 U.S. internet consumers ages 18–59; explicitly labeled 2015 |
| 77% said a website made a business appear more credible | Verisign 2015 U.S. Online Survey | Verified in source report | Historical perception measure, not a 2026 result or revenue measure |
| $199 / 365 ≈ $0.545 | Direct calculation | Verified | Presented as an annualized comparison, not daily billing |

The series intentionally makes no universal revenue-loss, ranking, traffic,
conversion, or sales claim. It says absence can create a verification and trust
gap or remove a business from consideration, with outcome depending on market,
demand, competition, and execution.

## Editorial acceptance

- Eight articles remain distinct by intent.
- Each article has at least 900 words and three recorded sources.
- Forbidden scope language count is zero across processed article copy.
- Every material numeric claim is dated, sourced, and accompanied by its
  methodology or limitation.
- The campaign CTA points to the complete offer or needs-led assessment; it
  does not default every website inquiry to $199.
