# FAMtastic Designs 17-day content and video sprint

## Goal

Create sustained attention for FAMtastic Designs and the Web Basics campaign,
move viewers into owned website and email journeys, and learn which objections,
audiences, and formats produce qualified actions. This is a controlled campaign,
not permission to publish unreviewed generated content or spend advertising money.

## Publishing model

Publish four **content moments** per day, not four identical posts on every
network. Each moment has one job and is adapted to the platform:

1. **Teach** — searchable or educational content.
2. **Challenge** — an objection, myth, or strong campaign position.
3. **Prove** — a demonstration, workflow, visual example, or transparent lesson.
4. **Invite** — assessment, offer explanation, FAQ, referral, or conversation.

Seventeen days produces 68 core content moments. Channel adaptation can create
approximately 200–270 placements across Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube
Shorts, Stories, and selected long-form YouTube without publishing the same
caption and creative everywhere.

## Daily rhythm

Suggested Eastern Time windows are starting hypotheses and must be adjusted
from observed reach, retention, clicks, and inquiries:

- 8:00 a.m. — Teach: carousel, diagram, FAQ, or 30–60 second explainer.
- 12:30 p.m. — Challenge: Reel/Short/TikTok objection hook.
- 5:30 p.m. — Prove: screen demonstration, build lesson, before/after, or process.
- 8:30 p.m. — Invite: story, poll, comment prompt, assessment, or offer CTA.

Do not automatically publish all four moments to every channel. Stories can
carry lighter reminders; Facebook can use a more complete explanation; TikTok
and Reels require native hooks and pacing; YouTube Shorts needs a searchable
title and description; long-form YouTube should deepen only the best topics.

## Video mix

Use several repeatable formats so the campaign does not resemble one faceless
template repeated 68 times:

- 24 kinetic typography and branded motion videos, 10–25 seconds.
- 14 photoreal small-business scenario videos, 15–35 seconds.
- 10 screen demonstrations or annotated product/workflow recordings.
- 8 optional HeyGen spokesperson explainers, 30–60 seconds.
- 8 founder voiceover or direct-camera clips when Fritz chooses to record.
- 3 YouTube explainers, 4–8 minutes, assembled from the strongest clusters.
- 1 day-17 campaign recap.

Faceless content is a format, not a channel strategy. A credible channel needs
recognizable FAMtastic opinions, demonstrations, recurring visual identity, and
eventually human presence. AI avatars must not pretend to be customers or imply
that a synthetic person is Fritz.

## Tool recommendation

### HeyGen — pilot, do not make it the whole system

Add HeyGen for an eight-video controlled pilot if the avatar and voice are
approved. It is useful for presenter-led explainers, consistent delivery, and
rapid script variation. It is not the best engine for diagrams, product demos,
kinetic typography, or every daily post. Generated scripts still pass Content
QA, SEO/Discovery QA, visual QA, and final approval before scheduling.

### Remotion — recommended motion-graphics foundation

Use Remotion for reusable React-based templates: hooks, captions, branded
transitions, equations, timelines, phone frames, diagrams, calls to action, and
9:16/1:1/16:9 exports. Keep a license review in the setup record. FAMtastic
currently qualifies for Remotion's published individual/small-company free-use
terms only while its organization size remains within those terms.

### Distribution — Postiz pilot or managed scheduler

Postiz is the strongest open-source candidate found for a scheduling pilot. It
supports many channels and has a public API, but self-hosting creates operational
and security responsibility. Any pilot must pin a current patched release,
review the AGPL implications, use official OAuth flows, start with test/private
posts, and monitor failed or stuck jobs. A managed scheduler is preferable when
reliability and platform support are worth more than infrastructure control.

TikTok and YouTube integrations still require platform authorization and review.
Unaudited TikTok Direct Post clients are restricted to private visibility;
unverified YouTube API projects upload privately. Therefore auto-posting should
be phased rather than treated as a one-click installation.

## Agent workflow

```text
Campaign brief
  -> content draft + source packet
  -> Content QA agent
  -> SEO/Discovery QA agent
  -> visual/video production
  -> visual and offer-logic QA
  -> approval queue
  -> scheduler
  -> delivery verification
  -> analytics and daily learning
```

### Content QA agent

The Content QA agent blocks publication when any of these fail:

- one clear audience, purpose, hook, and next action;
- accurate product scope, price, renewal, eligibility, and boundary language;
- no invented statistics, testimonials, urgency, scarcity, or results;
- no generic repeated body copy or unsupported technology bias;
- no mismatch between visual, spoken script, caption, landing page, and offer;
- readable captions, alt text, disclosures, and mobile-safe presentation;
- transactional/promotional distinction and brand voice;
- source packet supports every material factual claim.

Output: `pass`, `revise`, or `block`, with issue severity and an evidence trail.

### SEO and discovery QA agent

The SEO/Discovery QA agent evaluates product pages, articles, YouTube metadata,
and social discovery fields:

- intent, primary question, title/hook, entity clarity, and answer-first copy;
- unique title, description, canonical, structured-data eligibility, and links;
- product naming and offer details match the canonical product files;
- internal links connect the post to the correct article, FAQ, service, package,
  assessment, and next step;
- YouTube title, description, chapters, captions, thumbnail, playlist, and tags;
- platform-native keywords and hashtags without stuffing;
- UTM source, medium, campaign, content ID, and landing-page match;
- no duplicate/cannibalizing asset unless it has a distinct audience or intent.

SEO scores are internal heuristics, never claims about Google's ranking system.

## Automation states

Every content record moves through explicit states:

`idea -> briefed -> drafted -> content_qa -> seo_qa -> media_ready -> approved -> scheduled -> published -> verified -> measured -> learned`

A failure moves to `revision_required`, `delivery_failed`, or `blocked`. No
generated item may skip directly from `drafted` to `scheduled`. The scheduler
must be idempotent, record platform post IDs, retry only bounded transient
failures, and alert Fritz about exhausted or unmatched jobs.

## Rollout

1. Days -7 to -4: connect channels, build Remotion templates, create HeyGen
   pilot, define content schema, and verify analytics/UTMs.
2. Days -3 to -1: produce and QA days 1–5; load scheduler as private/draft;
   verify previews on actual phones.
3. Days 1–3: assisted publishing; Fritz approves daily release batch.
4. Days 4–7: auto-publish only low-risk, preapproved posts; manually review
   avatar, claims, pricing, and product-specific posts.
5. Days 8–17: adapt hooks and formats from observed retention, clicks, replies,
   and qualified actions. Do not continue a weak format merely to complete the
   calendar.

## Measurement

Use a content ID shared by the manifest, asset filenames, UTM content value,
scheduler record, and analytics event. Review daily:

- delivery success and failures;
- first-three-second hold and average watch percentage;
- saves, shares, meaningful comments, and profile actions;
- website sessions, engaged sessions, article depth, and CTA clicks;
- assessment starts/completions, lead acknowledgments, and qualified leads;
- checkout starts/completions where applicable;
- best hook, audience, objection, visual format, CTA, and channel.

The day-17 report should recommend what to stop, repeat, deepen into a blog or
YouTube guide, and convert into an evergreen automated sequence.

## Approval boundaries

Fritz approval remains required before connecting/publishing to real social
accounts, adopting paid HeyGen or scheduler plans, advertising spend, real
promotional sends, avatar/voice use, or changing product/legal promises.
