# Booked & Branded — $199 founding pilot

**Status:** Draft product proposal; not configured, published, purchasable, or
approved for outreach.

**Proposed product key:** `FAM-BOOKED-BRANDED-199`

**Proposed campaign profile:** `platform_independence`

**Primary audience:** Solo barbers, braiders, stylists, locticians, nail
professionals, and similar appointment businesses whose public customer path
currently depends on a marketplace or booking-platform profile.

## Product thesis

The customer already has a booking link. The problem is that the link does not
give the business a distinctive branded front door or a durable place to grow
beyond the platform.

Booked & Branded gives the operator:

- a custom mobile-first website under their business name;
- a small phone-friendly Booking Desk for daily work;
- a service menu, policies, portfolio, and directions in one owned experience;
- a simple booking-request path that does not require a full scheduler on day
  one;
- the business's own Cash App or existing payment QR displayed on the site; and
- a limited, moderated review showcase built from fresh customer submissions.

This is not a feature-for-feature Booksy replacement. It is the smallest useful
independent business system that can coexist with Booksy during transition and
grow through explicit additions.

The market position is **Booksy for discovery; your website for ownership**.
The operator does not have to abandon a marketplace that is still producing
new customers. Booked & Branded adds an owned brand, a direct-booking choice,
explicit first-party marketing consent, and a path for repeat customers to
return without making the marketplace the only front door.

This profile stays separate from the `first_site` campaign. A business belongs
here only when research verifies an active public appointment-platform profile,
an appointment-based operating model, and no independently hosted branded site.
The current ten-candidate review remains research material only; it must be
requalified for this profile, public contact provenance, suppression, and owner
approval before any import or proof generation.

## Why this segment is credible

Fritz reports strong anecdotal dissatisfaction among known Booksy users,
including Shay and four barbers. That is a promising founding-pilot group, not
a validated market statistic.

Booksy's official United States pricing page currently lists a $29.99 monthly
base subscription plus $20 monthly for each additional user. Its optional Boost
feature charges a one-time 30% commission on a Boost client's first visit, and
separate payment-processing rates apply. Booksy also includes materially more
than the proposed starter: full calendar tooling, reminders, marketing,
reports, staff management, payments, reviews, waitlists, memberships, and other
features. FAMtastic must compare scope honestly.

Square currently offers a no-monthly-fee solo appointments tier with processing
fees. Therefore the FAMtastic value proposition is not “the cheapest calendar.”
It is a distinctive owned brand experience, a connected customer path, and a
small operator surface that can evolve without rebuilding the website.

## Pluggable booking strategy

The website and operator experience remain consistent while the booking engine
can advance by evidence.

### Mode A — Platform bridge

The new branded site sends booking to the operator's current Booksy, Google,
Cal.com, or other booking link. This is the lowest-risk launch and keeps the
old calendar active while the new brand experience goes live.

### Mode B — FAMtastic request-to-book

The website accepts a preferred service and time. The owner confirms or
proposes another time from the Booking Desk. This is the recommended $199
pilot mode because it is useful, phone-manageable, and does not pretend to be a
complete real-time scheduling engine.

### Mode C — Connected live calendar

A later add-on can connect a supported third-party calendar or a proven native
availability service. This must pass conflict, timezone, cancellation,
reminder, availability, and recovery testing before being sold as instant
booking.

### Low-cost scheduler candidates

- **Google Calendar appointment schedules:** a personal Google account can
  support one booking page, and Google documents link and website-embed paths.
  It is a credible low-cost single-operator option, but initial schedule setup
  requires a desktop browser and several premium features require an eligible
  paid subscription. The business owns the Google account; FAMtastic does not
  hold its password.
- **Easy!Appointments:** the official project is GPL-3.0, self-hosted, and
  supports appointment/customer management, providers, services, rules,
  Google Calendar synchronization, and email notifications. It is a candidate
  for a later controlled self-hosted lane, not an automatic $199 dependency.
  Its current release and security advisories must be reviewed and a
  tenant/isolation, backup, update, notification, and recovery contract must
  pass before FAMtastic offers it in production.
- **FAMtastic request-to-book:** remains the smallest bounded starter because it
  can produce a real customer request and phone-managed response without
  claiming live availability or introducing a second unproven calendar.

## Payment and messaging boundary

The starter displays a customer-supplied Cash App QR or an existing QR from the
payment provider the business already uses. The payment stays directly between
the client, the business, and that provider.

- FAMtastic does not process, receive, settle, refund, or reconcile payment.
- FAMtastic does not collect card numbers, bank data, or merchant credentials.
- Payment-processing fees are paid directly by the business to its provider.
- Optional messaging fees are paid directly by the business to its provider.
- A future processing product would require a separate governed product,
  contract, implementation, proof, and customer authorization. It is not part
  of Booked & Branded.

## Booksy transition plan

1. **Mirror the public facts:** customer-approved services, prices, durations,
   policies, hours, portfolio, and business information.
2. **Launch bridge mode:** keep the Booksy booking path available while the
   branded site is tested.
3. **Pilot request mode:** owner handles a bounded number of FAMtastic booking
   requests from the phone.
4. **Validate:** confirm no lost requests, mobile usability, notification
   delivery, privacy, and the business-approved payment QR.
5. **Switch intentionally:** change the main booking destination only after the
   owner accepts the new workflow.
6. **Export by authority:** obtain client/appointment data through the
   operator's authorized export. Do not scrape platform data or copy reviews.

## Direct-booking incentive economics

The direct price or perk is selected by the business; it is not a FAMtastic
default and must not be advertised as guaranteed savings.

For illustration, if a first-time Boost customer books a $120 service, Booksy's
current 30% first-visit Boost fee is $36. The operator retains $84 before any
other fees. A $100 direct booking retains $100 before any other fees: $16 more
than that Boost-acquired first visit while giving the client a visible reason
to choose the owned path.

That comparison changes after the first Boost visit. Booksy currently states
that the second visit and beyond has 0% Boost commission. A permanent $100
direct price versus a $120 repeat Booksy price would then cost the operator $20
per repeat appointment before considering any other benefit. The incentive
therefore needs to earn its keep through retention, direct consent, easier
rebooking, lower platform dependence, or higher lifetime value.

Recommended experiments, chosen and approved by the operator:

1. **First direct rebook:** a one-time incentive for a returning client who
   books through the owned site.
2. **Direct loyalty rate:** a visible returning-client rate with written terms
   and a review date.
3. **Same price, better value:** preserve price and add a useful owner-approved
   perk instead of discounting.
4. **Service-specific bridge:** use an incentive only on services where margin,
   capacity, and repeat behavior support it.

Never exchange a discount, free service, reduced next-visit price, or other
benefit for a review. Do not market to a customer using platform-derived
contact information without the customer's applicable consent.

## The owned-client growth loop

1. A customer discovers the operator through Booksy, Google, social media, a
   referral, or the owned site.
2. The operator provides the service and, at an appropriate customer-approved
   moment, offers the owned booking page or QR for the next visit.
3. The customer chooses Booksy or direct booking. The owned path obtains clear
   consent before any marketing follow-up.
4. A completed direct appointment can trigger a fresh review request, referral
   invitation, and useful rebooking reminder, subject to consent and moderation.
5. Those approved reviews, referrals, and portfolio updates strengthen the
   owned site, creating more direct discovery and repeat business.
6. The operator reviews channel share and workload monthly, then keeps,
   reduces, or removes platform dependence based on evidence.

This loop does not scrape Booksy, copy reviews, secretly redirect customers, or
promise that a direct booking always costs less.

### Weekly pilot rhythm

The pilot may run a weekly analysis job only after it has lawful, consented
first-party events. The job reads aggregate channel, booking, completion,
rebooking, consent, review, referral, and response-time signals. It may prepare:

- a channel summary;
- a proposed direct-booking incentive adjustment;
- draft rebooking, review, referral, or portfolio content; and
- exceptions requiring owner attention.

It may not send a message, change a service price, publish content, activate an
offer, or switch a booking destination. Those remain explicit owner decisions.
Every run stores its input window, output, approval state, and last successful
position so it cannot repeat the same customer action.

## Personalized proof package

Each prospect receives exactly three private directions:

1. **Clean chair:** fast service selection, availability/request CTA, and
   restrained editorial portfolio.
2. **Signature work:** image-led personal brand, specialty services, and
   preparation/policy flow.
3. **Neighborhood authority:** trust, directions, returning-client shortcuts,
   approved reviews, and the business's own booking/payment QR.

Each proof includes the same real functional contract. Direction differences
are visual and compositional, not fake features.

### Reusable visual families

The template library should share one functional engine but not one visual
layout with color swaps. Initial families:

- **Barber authority:** blade geometry, athletic type, neighborhood credibility,
  fast service decisions.
- **Loc ritual / hair architecture:** coil systems, tactile material studies,
  consultation and care as a visual journey.
- **Glam editorial:** high-fashion scale contrast, restrained interface chrome,
  portfolio-led service discovery.
- **Neighborhood studio:** local warmth, directions, trust, and returning-client
  shortcuts.
- **Modern bilingual barber:** bilingual hierarchy, crisp navigation, service
  clarity, and culturally specific imagery without stereotypes.
- **Clean luxury suite:** quiet space, premium typography, consultation,
  privacy, and considered motion.

Every family must define its typography composition, type scale, motif,
texture, shape language, color behavior, motion thesis, service hierarchy, and
phone Booking Desk skin. Native HTML/CSS owns all readable text and controls.

### Premium visual production rule

For every premium image or graphic position:

1. Generate **three materially different candidates**, not three near-identical
   seed variations.
2. Review candidates against composition, cultural fit, subject integrity,
   usable negative space, motif, texture, contrast, and responsive crops.
3. Select one and perform a finishing pass: tonal balance, local contrast,
   cleanup, crop, sharpening, artifact removal, and Adobe finishing when the
   available tool and license are proven.
4. Retain all three candidates, rejection/selection rationale, before/after
   artifact, provider/model, prompt, cost/usage, and hashes in Build DNA.

A paid run still requires its own budget authorization. Provider execution is
not visual approval, and generated text or UI never replaces native page copy.
