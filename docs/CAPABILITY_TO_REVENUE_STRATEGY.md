# Capability-to-Revenue Strategy

**Strategy baseline:** August 11, 2026  
**Purpose:** Translate FAMtastic Designs' proven capabilities into customer demand, understandable offers, appropriate billing, and long-term customer value.

## Strategic position

FAMtastic Designs should not market isolated technical features. It should market the business problems those capabilities solve and the outcomes customers value.

The revenue system is:

1. Capability
2. Customer problem
3. Educational content
4. Clear offer
5. Proof
6. Relevant call to action
7. Purchase or consultation
8. Delivery
9. Support, retention, and relevant upsell

Technology supports the promise, but the customer-facing message should begin with the result. For example, “We build customer portals using React and Drupal” becomes “Give your customers one place to see purchases, projects, files, support requests, instructions, and next steps.”

## Customer-facing capability categories

### 1. Get Found

**Capabilities:** Website strategy, design and engineering, SEO, content publishing, analytics, and campaign landing pages.

**Customer promise:** Help the right people discover your business and understand why they should choose you.

### 2. Get Customers

**Capabilities:** Lead capture, quote requests, website intake, campaign tracking, personalized proofs, and sales follow-up automation.

**Customer promise:** Turn website visitors into real inquiries—and make sure opportunities do not get lost.

### 3. Get Paid

**Capabilities:** Ecommerce, Stripe payments, products and add-ons, discounts, customer-specific pricing, subscriptions, and renewals.

**Customer promise:** Make it easy for customers to buy, pay, and continue doing business with you.

### 4. Serve Customers

**Capabilities:** Customer portals, projects, files, approvals, support cases, knowledge bases, domains, hosting, and notifications.

**Customer promise:** Give customers a professional place to manage everything they have with your business.

### 5. Grow and Automate

**Capabilities:** Workflow automation, AI agents, analytics, retention campaigns, contextual upsells, referrals, and renewal journeys.

**Customer promise:** Reduce repetitive work, keep customers longer, and create more revenue from relationships already earned.

These categories should become customer navigation paths, content pillars, assessment outcomes, offer families, and portal recommendation categories.

## Demand-generation content strategy

The blog should demonstrate first-hand experience with systems FAMtastic Designs has actually built. Each article should:

1. Name a real business problem.
2. Explain why it happens.
3. Show what a better system looks like.
4. Give the reader a logical next action.

This approach follows Google's guidance to publish useful, original, people-first content that demonstrates real experience rather than mass-producing summaries for search traffic.

## Product-explanation standard

Every future product or service page—and every product-onboarding record—should answer:

1. What problem does this solve?
2. Who is it for?
3. What result should the customer expect?
4. What is included?
5. What is excluded?
6. What information is required from the customer?
7. What happens after purchase?
8. How long should delivery take?
9. What is paid once?
10. What renews?
11. What optional additions are available?
12. What proof or examples can FAMtastic show?
13. What questions do buyers commonly ask?
14. What is the next action?

This prevents adding a product that Commerce can sell but the website cannot explain or operations cannot deliver consistently.

## Billing model

### Defined project fee

Use for bounded deliverables such as landing pages, basic websites, redesigns, ecommerce setup, portal implementation, AI-agent setup, and workflow implementation.

### Paid assessment or discovery

Use when the correct solution cannot be known before discovery: process assessment, automation assessment, ecommerce discovery, portal planning, AI readiness, and conversion audits. The fee may be credited toward an approved build when appropriate.

### Monthly or annual recurring service

Use only when FAMtastic continues providing clear value such as hosting, maintenance, support, analytics, SEO work, content management, AI-agent monitoring, automation monitoring, email campaigns, or conversion optimization.

Recurring billing must state the continuing service, price, cadence, renewal, cancellation behavior, and included limits.

### Usage-based charges

Use where underlying cost changes with consumption, including AI usage, email volume, SMS, storage, and premium third-party services. Include a clear allowance where practical and explain overages before purchase.

### Add-ons

Use for bounded additions such as extra pages, copywriting, branding, scheduling, specialized forms, ecommerce features, AI agents, lead automation, analytics, and local SEO.

### Custom proposal

Use for unusual integrations, complex operations, migrations, multiple locations, specialized compliance, or substantial custom development.

## Proof-led marketing

FAMtastic Designs' own platform should serve as the first case study:

- How its lead workflow operates.
- What happens after a customer purchases.
- What customers receive in the portal.
- How requests are protected from being lost.
- How a purchase becomes intake, delivery, support, and retention.
- What the synthetic proof agent verifies.
- What was learned while building the system.

The principle is: prove a capability internally, document the proof, explain the business value, package the repeatable process, and then sell it.

## Recommended first campaign

### Your Website Should Do More Than Exist

The campaign sequence:

1. What a business website should actually do.
2. How a website captures a lead.
3. What should happen after the lead arrives.
4. How customers purchase online.
5. What a customer portal does.
6. How automation prevents lost opportunities.
7. How analytics shows what is working.
8. When AI adds real value.

The primary CTA should be a Website Needs Assessment. The assessment should recommend one or more paths based on actual needs:

- Basic online presence
- Lead-generating website
- Ecommerce website
- Customer portal
- Automation system
- AI-enabled experience
- Custom combination

The assessment must not force every customer into the $199 offer.

## Next strategic artifact: Capability-to-Revenue Matrix

For every capability, record:

- Customer problem
- Customer-facing benefit
- Evidence currently available
- Target customer
- Educational blog topics
- Lead magnet or assessment
- Primary CTA
- Potential standalone offer
- Potential add-on
- One-time billing opportunity
- Recurring billing opportunity
- Delivery requirements
- Dependencies
- Maturity and proof limitations
- Natural follow-on offers

Use the matrix to prioritize blog publication, product pages, site CTAs, assessments, Commerce products, portal upsells, and capabilities requiring more proof.

## Governing principle

Sell the outcome, teach the system, show the proof, and bill according to the work performed and the continuing value delivered.
