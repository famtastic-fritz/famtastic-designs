# Tighten Up Your Locs — business growth plan

**Purpose:** turn Shay’s intake into a sequence of useful business moves, then attach each move to a concrete FAMtastic service or capability. This is an owner/secure-workspace deliverable—not public website copy.

## The operating thesis

The website is not the business plan. It is Shay’s branded home base for discovery, availability, requests, repeat care, and growth. The goal is not to keep sending people back to a marketplace profile; it is to give Shay a client relationship and operating system she owns over time.

The first objective is not “more traffic.” It is to make the next action easy for a person at each Sisterlock stage and make rebooking easier for established clients.

## The first 90 days — measured experiments, not promises

### Days 0–30: build a trustworthy home

1. Confirm the business spelling, final domain, phone/text destination, hours, address/map permission, services, preparation notes, policies, and approved portfolio images.
2. Launch the site with four mobile actions always reachable: **Book**, **Contact**, **Find Us**, and owner-only **Desk** access.
3. Launch the owned request-to-book path first: clients can see a request window or tell Shay what time works, and Shay confirms manually from her phone.
4. Establish a small measurement baseline: availability views, request starts and completions, direction taps, and the source that brought each visitor.

**FAMtastic seed:** domain + hosting/SSL setup, branded site, protected request route after approval, forwarding-email setup where selected, analytics event plan, owner handoff.

### Days 31–60: make rebooking easier

1. Add a returning-client path that opens the direct request/rebook routine in one tap.
2. If Shay wants it, test a single owner-controlled “opening” request—not a live calendar promise. Shay publishes an invitation, reviews requests, then confirms manually.
3. Collect only owner-authorized photos, captions, and real client words. No copied marketplace reviews and no made-up testimonials.
4. Review which client stage produces the most questions and improve that one page/section first.

**FAMtastic seed:** mobile Booking Desk configuration, request workflow, approved portfolio publishing, contact-form routing, analytics review, and a scoped rebook/availability experiment.

### Days 61–90: turn proof into a repeatable growth loop

1. Choose one measurable growth question—for example, “Do returning clients use the branded rebook button?” or “Do availability requests create qualified conversations?”
2. Build one small, useful content series around that question: preparation, first consultation, establishment, maintenance, or retightening rhythm. Teach; do not publish generic salon filler.
3. Use the measured actions to decide whether the next investment is local discovery, a rebooking/availability workflow, a portfolio/story section, referral support, or a calendar integration.

**FAMtastic seed:** business-growth review, content/campaign foundation, attribution reporting, contextual next-service recommendation, and scoped automation discovery when the evidence supports it.

## Owner control from a phone

The finished owner desk should make only high-value changes easy on mobile:

- turn a request window on or off;
- review a new request and mark it responded, declined, or closed;
- update a temporary availability note, service detail, or approved photo;
- copy/share the branded domain or a booking link;
- see trustworthy action counts and exceptions—not vanity metrics;
- open the next best action, such as “confirm hours” or “review this request.”

It must **not** silently connect Google Calendar, publish availability, send messages, change payments, or register a domain. Those are separate owner approvals.

## Launch decisions Shay needs to make

1. Is `TightenUpYourLocs.com` the desired brand domain, and is it available/approved for registration or should an existing domain be connected?
2. Should the launch begin with direct request-to-book only, or should FAMtastic connect a reviewed calendar provider after the request flow is proven?
3. What phone/text/email destination can appear publicly?
4. Is the public address accurate for the branded site and map?
5. Which real photos, service descriptions, preparation notes, policies, and reviews are approved for use?
6. Which one growth experiment should be measured first: retightening rebooks, availability openings, first consultations, local discovery, or portfolio-led trust?

## Success indicators

The initial report should show facts, not a fake “growth score”:

- branded-site visits by source;
- availability views and request-window selections;
- contact/request starts and completed requests;
- direction/map taps;
- owner response time for requests once the inbox is enabled;
- which content or service path led to the action.

Those indicators reveal where to improve. They do not guarantee bookings, revenue, ranking, or growth.
