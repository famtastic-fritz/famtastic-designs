# Alex chair-side conversion plan

Status: owner sales aid; not a customer contract, guarantee, or activated
private offer.

Prototype:
`https://famtasticdesigns.com/showcase/booked-and-branded-pilot/alex-touch-prototype/`

Owner walkthrough:
`https://famtasticdesigns.com/showcase/booked-and-branded-pilot/alex-touch-prototype/owner/`

## The objective

Earn one clear decision: Alex wants FAMtastic to turn the approved prototype
into his owned one-page website foundation. Do not try to sell every future
integration in the barber chair. The first win is the $199 foundation and a
short fact-confirmation session.

## Five-minute chair-side sequence

### 1. Start with respect, not software

> Alex, I built this around your work and your chair. I did not make you look
> like the owner of the shop. I made the reputation you have already earned the
> destination.

Open the public prototype. Show only the hero, reputation, real-work collage,
and request button. Ask:

> Does this feel like you—or is there a different side of your work you want
> people to feel first?

This creates participation before price.

### 2. Show the owned advantage

> Instagram helps people discover you. The shop gives you a chair. This gives
> your name one place clients can remember, share, and come back to—without
> asking you to abandon what already works.

Do not claim the site will increase revenue. Ask which client path is annoying
today: answering the same questions, explaining prices, finding the shop,
requesting times, or showing recent work.

### 3. Reveal the phone control

Open the owner walkthrough. Demonstrate only four actions:

1. confirm a sample request;
2. change one service price;
3. turn one service off;
4. show where an approved booking or payment link/QR can live.

Then say:

> The point is not to give you another complicated system. It is to let you
> control the basics from your phone and keep every upgrade optional.

### 4. Make the founding offer

> I can turn the approved direction into your one-page site for $199. That
> includes the first year of basic hosting, the standard domain treatment, one
> proof cycle, one revision round, and the founding one-operator request desk
> we just walked through. We confirm the exact services, hours, location, and
> images before anything launches.

Disclosure:

- basic hosting after the included first year is $9.99 per month only after
  separate authorization;
- domain renewal is separate at the disclosed registrar price;
- advanced scheduling is an optional $149 setup;
- messaging, payment-provider, premium-domain, and other third-party costs are
  optional and paid directly to those providers; and
- no bookings, revenue, savings, or platform exit are guaranteed.

### 5. Ask for the smallest useful commitment

> If this direction feels right, let us lock the $199 foundation today. I only
> need twenty minutes with you to confirm the real services, hours, shop
> details, images, and the link you want clients to use first.

If he says yes, use the normal account-protected Web Basics checkout at
`https://famtasticdesigns.com/buy`, then record the founding Booking Desk scope
in the accepted intake/project record before work begins. Do not take an
off-system payment or promise an unrecorded integration.

## Objection card

| Alex says | Response | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| “I already have Instagram.” | “Keep it. Instagram is discovery; this is the owned place every profile, QR, and returning client can point to.” | “What question do people DM you most?” |
| “The shop already has a page.” | “That represents the shop. This represents your chair, your services, and your returning clients without claiming you own the shop.” | “Would you want the shop link included too?” |
| “I use Booksy/a calendar already.” | “Keep what works. We can point the site to it first, or test the request desk beside it.” | “Which path do you want new clients to try first?” |
| “I do not want another monthly bill.” | “The build is $199 and includes year one. The $9.99 hosting renewal starts only after year one and only if you separately authorize it.” | “Would that remove the concern?” |
| “I need to think.” | “Fair. Tell me which part you are deciding: the look, the workflow, the price, or whether now is the right time.” | Address only the named concern. |

## Follow-up if he does not buy in the chair

Send one message with the two public URLs, the exact $199 scope, the renewal
disclosure, and a single question: “Which one change would make this feel ready
to become yours?” Do not start a multi-message automation without consent.

## Conversion evidence to record

- prototype viewed;
- public direction reaction;
- owner-console actions demonstrated;
- objections in Alex's own words;
- decision: yes, follow-up, or no;
- accepted facts still needed;
- checkout or next-conversation destination; and
- permission state for any future message.

The conversation is a founding-customer discovery session even if it does not
close. Preserve the feedback; do not manufacture a success story.
