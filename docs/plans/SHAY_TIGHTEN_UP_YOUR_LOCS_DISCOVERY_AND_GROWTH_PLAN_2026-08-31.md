# Tighten Up Your Locs — discovery and growth plan

Status: implementation-ready private interview; no production invitation,
customer account, booking integration, payment setup, proof, or publication has
been executed.

## Customer path

```text
Owner-reviewed exact-email invitation
  -> private one-question-at-a-time discovery link
  -> completed answers saved against a prospect record
  -> same-email account registration and verification
  -> account-owned website request draft
  -> owner scope/proof review
  -> core three proofs + explicit optional three-direction expansion
  -> selected direction, scoped offer, payment, build, approval, launch
```

The invitation cannot trigger the right-hand stages. Account verification only
creates a saved draft; it does not send a proof, charge a card, change Booksy,
use a payment account, register a domain, or publish a website.

## Interview outcomes

The interview captures the facts needed to build a real appointment-business
site:

- service menu, specialties, ideal clients, sales goal, local service area,
  availability, policies, and booking friction;
- current Booksy link and preferred bridge/request-to-book strategy;
- payment-display preference without merchant credentials or payment data;
- logo/brand status, colors, materials, references, visual intensity, owned
  assets, and per-file AI-use consent;
- local competitors, Google Business Profile status, genuine review sources,
  and frequent customer questions that can become helpful FAQs, posts, and
  future blog topics.

## Sales research after consent

This is a research sequence, not a revenue guarantee:

1. Verify the business name, service area, hours, phone, website/booking
   destination, and category on Google Business Profile. Google allows a
   business to manage booking links and designate a preferred link; use the
   client-approved branded page or Booksy bridge only after launch QA.
2. Compare local public search results and client-approved competitors for the
   actual booking questions: retightening cadence, preparation, service
   duration, policy clarity, specialties, location, and proof of work.
3. Build a customer-owned service/menu page and local-business structured data
   from only customer-approved facts. Validate it after deployment; markup does
   not promise a rich result.
4. Turn the client’s recurring questions into a short FAQ and content backlog.
   Publish only owner-approved, factual, brand-safe content.
5. Use fresh genuine review requests and respond to reviews; never offer an
   incentive in exchange for a review or copy/re-publish Booksy reviews without
   the client’s authority.
6. Track source, booking-link click, request-to-book, completed appointment,
   rebook, payment-link/QR view, and approved-review events. Optimize only
   after a real baseline exists.

## Booksy and payment boundary

Start in Booksy bridge mode unless Shay chooses the bounded request-to-book
pilot. There is no Booksy client scraping, review migration, credentials
collection, calendar sync, payment processing, settlement, or reconciliation.
The site may display an owner-approved destination/QR supplied by Shay; the
payment remains directly between her client and her chosen provider.

## Source notes

- Google Business Profile allows a business to manage booking links and set a
  preferred link; availability is governed by Google and the business profile.
- Google Business Profile guidance permits genuine review requests but forbids
  incentives for reviews.
- Google local-business structured-data guidance supports accurate visible
  business details but does not guarantee a search feature.
- Booksy’s public business materials describe online booking, rebooking,
  reminders, and social/Google booking channels. This informs the bridge
  decision but does not authorize a Booksy integration.
