# FAMtastic lean social-presence production process v1

## Purpose

This is the reusable production recipe behind the `AND IF IT IS?` result. The
campaign is the first golden example; it is not the process itself.

Use this process when FAMtastic needs one deep, coherent social identity and a
small campaign hub quickly. Use the separate six-direction website-proof
contract when a customer needs to compare one restrained, one medium, and four
ultra-FAMtastic website directions.

## Callable outcome

The intended callable routine is:

```text
social_presence.generate.v1(brief_id)
```

The routine accepts a normalized brief, resolves capabilities and providers at
runtime, creates a governed source package, and stops at the applicable human
approval boundary. A stage declares what it needs; it does not name a model.

## Input contract

The normalized brief must include:

- organization or concept name;
- business objective and desired customer action;
- primary and secondary audiences;
- geographic and cultural context;
- brand personality and FAMtastic intensity;
- approved source material, reference images, colors, and existing marks;
- claims that require verification;
- prohibited claims, marks, people, imagery, or subjects;
- channel targets and required aspect ratios;
- rights, consent, affiliation, and research boundaries;
- publishing authority and the human approver.

Missing commercial, legal, identity, or rights facts remain unresolved. A
creative worker cannot invent them.

## Output contract

Every completed run must preserve:

1. one verbatim and one normalized brief;
2. sourced research and rights boundaries;
3. one campaign thesis expressed as a memorable tension and response;
4. one art-direction system covering type, color, pattern, texture, material,
   depth, motion, image framing, and reusable character or graphic language;
5. two purposeful original images: a story-led hero and a reusable character
   or visual anchor;
6. one responsive campaign hub or mini-site;
7. three editable social-card layouts;
8. six governed draft content records;
9. desktop, phone, and card screenshots;
10. browser and visual-quality results;
11. provider, model, prompt, duration, retry, cost, hash, and reviewer records;
12. an approval-gated publication packet and permanent evidence package.

More assets may be requested by the brief. They are not generated by default.
The lean baseline deliberately spends creative effort on two strong images
rather than a large batch of interchangeable artwork.

## Nine-stage production flow

### 1. Normalize the brief

Validate required facts, separate supplied facts from recommendations, record
the FAMtastic intensity, and create stable run and artifact identifiers.

### 2. Research the truth and the boundary

Research the business, audience, cultural context, competitive language, and
useful history before writing copy or image prompts. Record sources, retrieval
time, disputed claims, trademark or affiliation risk, and what must not be
implied.

### 3. Find the campaign thesis

Reduce the research to one defensible human truth, one memorable tension or
question, one response, and one desired action. Reject generic themes that
could fit an unrelated business by changing only the name.

### 4. Design the visual grammar

Specify typography as composition rather than decoration. Specify surface
depth through patterns, textures, materials, shadows, atmosphere, and edge
treatment. Define an original character or graphic device when it strengthens
recognition without copying a protected mascot or mark.

### 5. Preflight and route capabilities

Resolve research, copy, image generation, web composition, browser QA, and
visual review through the capability router. Record the selected provider and
model after resolution. Fail closed when a required capability is unavailable;
never silently downgrade artwork or review quality.

### 6. Generate the two key images in parallel

Each prompt must define story, subject, setting, framing, material, lighting,
negative space, intended crop, prohibited marks, and known failure modes. Keep
the exact prompt and provider receipt. A failed candidate may be retried within
the declared policy; every retry remains visible in the ledger.

### 7. Assemble deterministically

Build the responsive hub, three social cards, and six draft records from the
approved direction. Navigation, disclosures, CTA behavior, filenames, output
structure, image derivatives, and manifest fields are deterministic. Creative
models do not own publishing, customer identity, pricing, or approval state.

### 8. Prove the experience

Run desktop and phone browser checks for route success, asset loading,
horizontal overflow, accessibility basics, console or page errors, working
local interactions, disclosure visibility, and publication-boundary visibility.
Then score concept specificity, first-impression impact, typography, surface
depth, business relevance, mobile usability, accessibility, conversion clarity,
and emotional response. Self-review must be labeled as self-review.

### 9. Approve, publish, and retain

Require explicit human approval for public publication, social-account access,
or posting. Publish atomically to an exact unlisted or public destination,
verify it anonymously, and retain the full evidence package. Preserve strong
unused structures and art only when rights allow; strip customer copy, identity,
and private media before adding anything to a reusable inspiration library.

## Lean time and cost discipline

The first golden example took 13 minutes 03 seconds from its first paid image
request through passing browser QA. Research time was not instrumented and is
therefore excluded. This is an observed benchmark, not a customer promise.

The target operating shape is:

- research and concept first;
- two parallel image calls;
- one deterministic composition pass;
- one browser pass;
- one bounded repair pass;
- one recorded visual decision.

Do not add another agent, stage, or framework unless a measured failure shows a
capability the current routine lacks. Refinement that benefits only the selected
client direction belongs after selection, not in every preview candidate.

## Provider-neutral image rule

Image generation is a capability with three separate records:

```text
capability -> transport/provider -> resolved model
```

The first golden run resolved to OpenArt as transport and GPT Image 2 as model.
Direct OpenAI Image API or Responses API can resolve the same model. Gemini,
MuAPI, a specialized image service, or a local model are substitutions and must
pass the same prompt and quality benchmark before promotion. A fallback is
declared, observable, and reviewable; it is never silent.

## Productization path

The recommended public expression is a two-surface release, not a case study
that consumes or interrupts the actual audience experience:

1. keep the working experience as an unlisted proof while rights are reviewed;
2. publish the approved audience experience on its own clean public route, with
   functional interactions and only the disclosures the audience needs;
3. publish a separate FAMtastic Lab DNA page explaining the challenge, research-first
   method, design system, two-image constraint, QA, and measured build window;
4. link the experience and Lab DNA in both directions without letting process
   commentary dominate the audience experience;
5. publish a supporting blog article about why research, typography, texture,
   and original visual language outperform deterministic cookie-cutter pages;
6. route the Lab CTA to a clearly scoped social-presence or campaign-sprint intake;
7. measure experience engagement separately from Lab/process engagement, CTA
   starts, and qualified leads.

Suggested route family:

```text
/and-if-it-is
/lab/and-if-it-is
/blog/research-first-ai-social-presence
/services/ai-brand-campaign-sprint
```

Names and routes are recommendations only. Public promotion remains subject to
brand, rights, affiliation, copy, and publishing approval.

Implementation status on 2026-08-19: the audience experience is live at
