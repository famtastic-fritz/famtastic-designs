# Business Email on Your Own Domain: What It Actually Takes

If your invoice says yourbusiness.com but your reply-to address says
yourbusiness@gmail.com, that mismatch is doing quiet damage. A customer
comparing you to someone else notices which one looks like an established
business and which one looks like a side hustle — and a free-tier email
address is the tell. The fix is smaller than most owners expect: it's a
plumbing job, not a rebuild.

## What "plumbing" actually means here

Getting mail flowing correctly from you@yourbusiness.com is three
mechanical steps, in order:

1. **DNS records that prove you're allowed to send.** Your domain's DNS needs specific records (the kind mail servers check automatically) that say "this mail provider is authorized to send mail as this domain." Without them, messages you send are more likely to land in spam or get rejected outright.
2. **A mailbox provider connected to your domain.** This is the actual inbox — Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, or whatever mailbox subscription you already have or choose. The domain and the mailbox are two separate things that have to be pointed at each other correctly.
3. **A real send/receive test.** Not a guess that it's probably working — an actual message sent and received before anyone calls it done.

None of this touches your website's design, your existing inbox contents, or
anything else you already have running. It's a narrow, defined task with a
clear finish line.
