# Real Answers to the Questions We Actually Get

Most of what stops someone from asking a question out loud isn't the question. It's having to find someone to ask, then asking in front of a stranger.

We built a recurring format around this problem: one person, one setting, one real question answered directly on camera — eight short films, each answering one question we have genuinely been asked, each linking straight to the published post that goes deeper. Nothing below is invented for the format. The films sit further down this page; the same eight answers are written out here first, in full, for anyone who would rather read than watch.

## Do I have to leave my booking app?

No. If a booking app is filling your calendar, it's doing its job, and a channel that brings paying work isn't a problem to solve. [The real question is what happens if it ever stops](https://famtasticdesigns.com/blog/do-you-have-to-leave-the-booking-app/) — a policy change, a billing lapse, a category reshuffle — and what's left standing afterward is only what you own outside it.

## Who actually owns my client list?

You can usually see it, and often export some of it. But access is a permission a platform grants you, and that isn't the same thing as owning the list yourself. [What any specific platform actually lets you do](https://famtasticdesigns.com/blog/who-owns-your-client-list-booking-app/) is set by the agreement you accepted, and reading it is the only reliable way to know.

## Do you guarantee Google rankings?

No. Nobody honest does, because no outside vendor controls the algorithm that decides a ranking. A vendor who promises a specific position is either promising something outside their control or narrowing that promise later. [What we actually build](https://famtasticdesigns.com/blog/do-you-guarantee-google-rankings/) is the real technical foundation a search engine needs to evaluate a site at all, shown as proof rather than asserted as a promise.

## What does the $199 actually include?

One focused website, first-year hosting, and your domain — new, or one you already own. [That is the whole bundle](https://famtasticdesigns.com/blog/199-website-inclusions-and-boundaries/), stated once rather than discovered later. After year one, hosting is $9.99 a month, and it only starts once you separately approve it; domain renewal is billed on its own line, at whatever the registrar actually charges.

## Why does "DM me for pricing" cost you bookings?

Picture a shop with no price tags. Everything in it is good, and the owner knows the stock cold — but to learn what anything costs, a stranger has to find the owner and ask out loud in front of other people. [Some will do it](https://famtasticdesigns.com/blog/how-much-do-you-charge-dms-costs-bookings/); most will pick it up, put it back, and leave without speaking. A profile that says "DM for pricing" is that shop.

## What's actually wrong with just using Linktree?

Nothing, if it's routing people somewhere — it's fast, free, and solves the one-link problem cleanly. Keep it if it's currently working. But [it's a hallway, not a room](https://famtasticdesigns.com/blog/linktree-vs-real-website-what-you-trade-away/): it moves people in the right direction and then has nothing for them once they arrive — no prices, no proof, no address that's actually theirs.

## Why is a Gmail address costing me business?

A Gmail address and a Linktree page both work, and neither one is yours. [You don't control what shows up around your links](https://famtasticdesigns.com/blog/why-running-business-on-gmail-and-linktree-costs-revenue/), and if the platform changes its layout or policy, your booking flow changes with it. When someone has to message you just to ask what you charge, some of them will book with whoever answered first.

## What happens after the first year of hosting?

Nothing charges automatically, and nothing gets billed without your separate, explicit say-so first. [At month thirteen](https://famtasticdesigns.com/blog/what-happens-when-first-year-hosting-ends/), hosting becomes $9.99 a month for Web Basics sites — but only after you approve it — and domain renewal is billed on its own line, disclosed before you pay.

## The point of answering before you're asked

None of these eight answers is a sales pitch dressed as an FAQ. Several of them — leave the app, guarantee a ranking — are answered honestly with "no," because the honest answer sometimes disappoints the premise of the question. That's deliberate: a page that only ever says yes isn't answering anything, it's selling.

Every number in every answer above is a catalog fact, not a promise — the $199 figure, the $9.99 renewal, and the domain's own separate renewal cost are the only figures used anywhere on this page. No answer implies a ranking, a booking count, or a revenue outcome; where a guarantee genuinely doesn't exist, the answer says so plainly instead of quietly avoiding the question.
