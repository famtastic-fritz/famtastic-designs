# Brief — own-your-signal-not-a-rented-one

## Target reader
A business whose entire public presence runs through borrowed platforms — a Gmail inbox, a link-in-bio page, a booking app's directory — and has never had a reason to ask what happens if one of them changes its rules, its price, or whether it shows them at all.

## Search intent
Comparison/informational: "linktree vs website for small business", "do I need a website if I have Instagram", "who owns client list booking app", "does linktree show up on google". The reader is not shopping for a website yet — they are noticing, for the first time, that several tools they rely on are all rented.

## Key takeaway
A rented signal works right up until the platform that carries it changes something. A Gmail address, a link-in-bio page, and a booking app's client directory are all useful and all borrowed — none of them is an address a stranger can find, remember, and return to on terms the business itself controls. Keeping the booking app is not the issue; the issue is having nothing of your own alongside it. One owned page, at a domain the business holds, does not disappear when a platform's rules do.
