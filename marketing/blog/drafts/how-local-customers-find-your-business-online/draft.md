# How Local Customers Actually Find Your Business Online

If someone nearby searches for what you do and you don't show up, the cause
is almost never a mystery algorithm — it's usually one missing layer in a
three-layer system. Local search runs on structured data, business profiles,
and analytics working together. Fix the layer that's actually broken and the
rest starts pulling its weight.

## The three layers, plainly

1. **Structured data.** This is the machine-readable information that tells search engines exactly what your business is, what you offer, and where you serve customers — separate from the plain-English copy a visitor reads. Search engines use it to decide whether to show you for a given search at all.
2. **Business profiles.** Your listing on Google, Maps, and relevant directories: hours, address, phone number, map pin. These need to match each other and match your website exactly — a mismatched phone number or an old address is enough to make a listing untrustworthy or invisible.
3. **Analytics.** The layer that proves which of the above actually brought a visitor, rather than guessing. Without it, you're optimizing based on a feeling instead of what real traffic is doing.

## Why skipping a layer hurts the other two

These layers aren't independent. Structured data with no matching profile
looks incomplete to a search engine. A profile with no structured data
behind your website has nothing to confirm it. And without analytics,
there's no way to tell whether either one is working — you're left
guessing whether a change helped or did nothing at all. "Trust us, it
works" is not a measurement; a number you can check yourself is.
