# Brief — what-a-bookable-page-actually-needs

## Target reader
A service business whose bookings currently arrive through an app or a direct message, now considering a page of their own and unsure what actually belongs on it. They have seen other people's websites and have no way to tell which parts mattered.

## Search intent
Specification-seeking: "what should be on my stylist website", "what to put on a one page business website", "do I need prices on my website". They want a checklist they can trust, written by someone who will tell them what to leave out.

## Key takeaway
A bookable page has a different job from a marketplace profile, because the visitor arrives with a different question. On a marketplace the visitor has already chosen the platform and is comparing listings; on your own page the visitor is deciding whether to hire you at all. Everything on the page should serve one of two decisions — can this person do the specific thing I want, and can I trust them with it. Work organized the way a client shops, a price or the thing that determines the price, one obvious way to start, and the unglamorous location, hours, and policy details do that job. A full booking system does not have to be there on day one.

## Series position
Order 12 of "The Small-Business Website Strategy Series". Order 11 established why a profile you do not control cannot be steered; this is the first constructive post, specifying what the page you do control has to contain. Written for any app-booked service business, not for a named trade.

This overlaps `parts-of-a-one-page-business-website` (order 4 of The 55 Cents a Day Website Series, live) and must not restate it. That post is the general anatomy of a one-page business site. This one is scoped to the booking decision specifically: it is organized around the two questions a stranger is deciding rather than around page sections, and it covers portfolio organization, price ranges, and the form-versus-scheduling call, which the anatomy post does not. The two are cross-linked so a reader can move between them rather than reading the same thing twice.
