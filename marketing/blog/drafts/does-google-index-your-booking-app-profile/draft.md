# Does Google Index Your Booking App Profile?

Usually, yes. And it still is not a web presence of your own. Both halves of that sentence are true at the same time, and the gap between them is where a lot of small businesses quietly lose work.

## What indexing actually is

Three words get used interchangeably and they're three different steps.

- **Crawling** is a search engine following links around the web and reading pages, the way a very fast reader with no opinions would.
- **Indexing** is filing a page it has read, so the page can be shown to someone later. A page that is never indexed can never appear in a result.
- **Ranking** is deciding which of the indexed pages to show first for a particular search.

A booking-app profile usually clears the first two. Marketplaces want their pages read and filed, because that is how the marketplace gets found. So your profile is often sitting in the index right now.

The trouble starts at the third step, and at a fourth thing nobody names.

## Whose page is it, actually

Every page on the web lives at an address, and the memorable part of that address — the yourbusiness.com part — is the **domain**. The domain is the unit search engines treat as an identity. Reputation, in the loose sense that matters here, accumulates to a domain over time.

Your profile lives on the app's domain. Every visit it earns, every link anyone points at it, every signal that the page is useful — all of that accrues to the platform. None of it accrues to your business name, because your business name does not have an address for it to accrue to.

That's not a complaint about any particular app. It is just how the arithmetic works when you are a page inside someone else's house.

## The four things you cannot do on a profile

This is the practical part, and it's the part that surprises people who assumed indexing was the whole game.

- **You cannot write the title.** The blue line in a search result usually comes from the page's own title, and on a marketplace that title follows the marketplace's template.
- **You cannot add structured data.** Structured data is a small block of machine-readable notes attached to a page — hours, address, price range, services — that helps a search engine describe a business accurately. On a profile, you get whatever fields the app decided to expose.
- **You cannot control the links out.** A page you own can point to your other pages, your booking flow, your portfolio. A profile points where the platform wants visitors to go, which is usually further into the platform.
- **You cannot keep the address.** If the app changes its URL structure, retires a feature, or removes your listing, the page and everything it had earned goes with it. You are not consulted.

Individually, none of them is dramatic. Together they describe something specific: a page that can be found, and cannot be steered.

## What a page you own does differently

A page on your own domain is not magic, and nobody should promise you it will outrank anything. What it does is put the four items above back in your hands.

You decide what the title says. You can carry structured data that describes your actual business. You choose where a visitor goes next. And the address keeps working as long as you keep renewing it, regardless of what any platform does.

There's also a second, less obvious difference. A profile answers one question: is this person on this platform.

A page of your own can answer the question a stranger is actually asking, which is whether you can do the thing they want and be trusted with it. Those aren't the same question. A template built for the first one rarely answers the second.

## So what should you actually do

Not delete anything. The app is finding you work, and a channel that produces bookings is not a problem to be solved.

The move is to stop having exactly one point of failure. Register a domain in your business name. Put one honest page on it that shows your work, says what you do, and gives a stranger a way to reach you. Let the app keep doing its job alongside it.
