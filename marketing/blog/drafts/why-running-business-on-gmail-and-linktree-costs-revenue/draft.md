# Why Gmail and Linktree Cost Your Business Revenue

If your business card, your Instagram bio, and your invoices all point to a @gmail.com address and a Linktree page instead of your own website, you are running your business on rented land — and paying for it in ways that don't show up on any bill.

## What "rented land" actually means

A @gmail.com address and a link-in-bio page both work. Neither one is yours. You don't own the domain, you can't control what shows up around your links, and if the platform changes its layout, pricing, or policies, your booking flow changes with it — without your input.

An owned domain (yourbusiness.com) and email address (you@yourbusiness.com) are different: they belong to your business, not to a platform's free tier.

## The real cost: the DM gap

Here's the actual mechanic that costs revenue, described plainly rather than with an invented statistic: when a potential customer has to message you "how much do you charge?" instead of seeing a price or an instant estimate, two things happen. They wait for your reply, and some of them book with whoever answered first — often a competitor with a website that already answered the question.

A Linktree page routes a visitor to your booking app or your Instagram — it does not answer "how much" or "when are you free" on its own. Every one of those steps is a chance for the customer to leave before they book.

## What switching to an owned hub changes

1. **Zero middleman booking fees.** A booking-app percentage cut disappears when the estimate and intake happen on a page you own.
2. **Instant credibility.** A customer typing your name and getting yourbusiness.com — not a linktr.ee/ page — reads as an established business, not a side hustle.
3. **Automatic quote estimates and intake.** A real site can present pricing and collect the details you need before the first message, instead of after.
