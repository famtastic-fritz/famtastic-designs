# What Website Maintenance Actually Covers

"Maintenance" gets used loosely enough that most customers can't say what
they're actually paying for each month. Here's the scope in plain terms, and
just as importantly, where it stops.

## What's included

Every month, a maintenance plan covers four specific things:

1. **Update checks.** The underlying software your site runs on gets checked and kept current, so known issues don't quietly pile up.
2. **Backup verification.** Not just "a backup exists somewhere" — actually confirming a recent backup is really there and usable if something goes wrong.
3. **Monitoring for anything broken.** A form that stopped submitting, a page that started erroring, a link that quietly broke — caught before a customer finds it first.
4. **Small content touches.** Hours changed, a staff name updated, a service line added to an existing page — the small edits that come up between bigger projects.

## What's not included

New page builds, redesigns, and marketing campaigns are not maintenance —
they're separate, scoped work. That boundary isn't a technicality; it's what
keeps the monthly price honest. If "maintenance" quietly expanded to cover
anything a customer asked for, the plan would either need to cost far more
or the response time would slip for everyone on it. Keeping the scope
narrow is what makes the price predictable.

## What response time actually means

FAMtastic's stated response target is normally within three business days —
that's a target, not a guaranteed resolution or completion time, and this
article won't pretend otherwise. Some fixes are quick; others depend on
what's actually wrong. The plan is honest about the target rather than
promising a number nobody could actually guarantee.
