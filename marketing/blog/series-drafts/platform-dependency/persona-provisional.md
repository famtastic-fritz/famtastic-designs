# Provisional reader persona — The booking-first owner

Status: `provisional; not interview-validated`

## Evidence basis

- The platform-dependency cluster plan and its six reader questions.
- FAMtastic's documented audience: owners and operators who need a credible
  digital presence and a managed customer path.
- Existing draft language about social profiles, booking tools, direct
  messages, services, pricing, and availability.

This is not a demographic target or a claim about a real customer. It is a
consistent editorial reference until interviews, intake patterns, or analytics
can validate or replace it.

## Working profile

**Name:** The Booking-First Owner
**One-line mindset:** “Keep the tool that brings me appointments; help me stop
losing the information and questions around it.”

**Context:** Runs an appointment-led local service from a phone. Uses a social
profile and a booking platform because both are fast to operate between client
work. Has little time for a long marketing project and does not want to be
told that their current setup was foolish.

**Tech comfort:** Can manage a profile, booking settings, messages, and simple
links. Wants plain language before a new form, domain, or automation is added.

## Goals

- Make it easy for a stranger to understand service, location, availability,
  and a usable price boundary.
- Keep the channel that produces new appointments.
- Reduce repeated DMs without replacing conversations that need judgment.
- Know what customer contact information is available, permitted, and kept.

## Frustrations

- A full inbox of the same questions while working with clients.
- Not knowing whether a booking fee pays for a new introduction or a repeat
  customer.
- Being told to abandon a platform that is currently helping.
- Vague “automation” promises that do not explain what information is needed.

## Observable behaviors to write for

- Chooses the smallest useful change before considering a rebuild.
- Checks a phone between appointments; skims headings before reading detail.
- Wants concrete prompts: “open your statement,” “read this clause,” or
  “write these four answers.”
- Treats a fixed price as a commitment, so needs conditions and boundaries
  explained in the same sentence.

## Editorial implications

1. Start with the practical answer, not platform criticism.
2. Define search, domains, contact capture, and automation in ordinary words.
3. Give a reversible next action before a paid solution.
4. State what an owned page does *not* guarantee: rankings, binding quotes,
   or automatic customer consent.
5. Keep the CTA useful and conditional: review the setup, compare options, or
   start an appropriately scoped conversation.

## Research gaps

- Interviews with operators using specific booking platforms.
- Search Console query data for this cluster.
- Analytics on which repeated questions appear in actual inquiries.
- Legal review of platform-specific customer-contact rules.
