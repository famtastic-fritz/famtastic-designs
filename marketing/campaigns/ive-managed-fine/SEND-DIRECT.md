# Send Direct — which film for which prospect

These six films are short on purpose. They are built to be **sent to one person**,
not only posted. Every one stands alone: it names the objection, concedes what is
true about it, answers it, and ends with the price and the address. Nobody needs
to have seen the other five.

**Pick the film that matches the sentence they actually said to you.** Sending
the wrong one reads as a pitch. Sending the right one reads as an answer.

## If they have not said anything yet

Lead with **Thirty Years**. It is the only one that opens by agreeing with them,
and it does not mention a price until the last five seconds. It is the film least
likely to be read as a sales call.

Do **not** lead with **Too Expensive** unless they raised money first. Answering
a price objection nobody made tells them what you assumed about them.

## What to say when you send it

Short. The film does the work.

> "You said you've managed fine for thirty years — you have. Twenty seconds,
> and it's not a pitch. Tell me if it's wrong."

Do not follow up the same day. This audience reads speed as pressure.

## Things that are true about every film, so you can answer follow-ups

- **The price is $199 for the first year**, about 55 cents a day. After the
  first year it is $9.99 a month, plus the cost of the domain. That renewal
  line is on screen in every film — you are not going to be caught out by it.
- **What $199 covers**: one focused page, one year of managed hosting, and the
  domain — a new one registered, or the one they already own connected up.
- **Business email and maintenance are separate products.** Do not let a
  conversation drift into implying they are included.
- **They see designs before they pay.** Three real directions built from their
  own business information, reviewed by a person, then they choose one, then
  checkout. If they hate all three they have paid nothing.
- **No delivery-time promise.** Do not say "48 hours." There is no published
  SLA behind it.
- **No ranking promise.** If they ask whether this puts them at the top of
  Google, the honest answer is no, and there is a live post that says so.

## What is deliberately not in any of these films

No countdown. No "limited time". No statistic. No competitor named. The words
"still", "finally" and "even you" appear nowhere in the campaign — they are the
words that turn a fair objection into an accusation, and this audience hears
them coming.
