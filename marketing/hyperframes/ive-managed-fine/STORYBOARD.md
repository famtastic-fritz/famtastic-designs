# Storyboard — I've Managed Fine

Six films, three beats each, one objection each. The beat shape never changes,
because the argument never changes: **state the objection in their words →
concede what is true → answer with a mechanism → the offer, with the renewal.**

Beat boundaries are not guessed. Each beat's narration is generated as its own
audio block (`scripts/build-narration.py`), and the picture cut sits half a gap
after the block ends, so every cut lands inside real silence. The measured table
is `narration/timing.json`; the numbers below come from it.

---

## f5 — "It's too expensive." · 23.825s

Plate: `pd-p` — a blank white card in a brass holder on a worn wooden counter,
with a small olive clip beside it. The plate already carries its own accent
incident, so the composition adds only one more.

| Beat | Window | Picture | Type | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 – 4.875 | Tall photographic card | "A WEBSITE IS / TOO EXPENSIVE." | "A website is too expensive. Here is the whole number." |
| 2 | 4.875 – 15.35 | Elevated angled panel, price at 236px — the largest object in the campaign; the brass holder returns as a closing band | $199 · "55 cents a day, first year" · 01 One page / 02 One year of managed hosting / 03 A domain that belongs to you | "One hundred and ninety-nine dollars. That is fifty-five cents a day for the first year. One page. One year of hosting. A domain that belongs to you." |
| 3 | 15.35 – 23.825 | A five-row terms table on paper, one row at a time | First year $199 · Every month after that $9.99 · The domain, billed separately · Business email, a separate product · Maintenance, a separate product · "THAT IS ALL OF IT." | "After the first year it is nine ninety-nine a month, plus the domain. Beat 3 is the point of this film. An audience that has been burned does not want
the price, it wants the *whole* price, and the terms table is the only object in
the campaign that shows every line at once.

## f6 — "I'm too close to retiring." · 21.5s

Plate: `pd-a1-tight` — a brass letter slot in a painted door above a worn stone
step. The address itself, which is exactly what the film argues about.

| Beat | Window | Picture | Type | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 – 4.775 | Full-bleed plate under a **diagonal** paper wedge, 1.00→1.05 push | "I'M TOO CLOSE / TO RETIRING." | "You are close to retiring. Then it matters more, not less." |
| 2 | 4.775 – 16.15 | Paper; the letter slot returns as a closing band | "THEN IT MATTERS MORE, / NOT LESS." · two columns, TRANSFERS (a domain, a page) against DOES NOT (thirty years of people knowing your name) · "THAT WALKS OUT / THE DOOR / WITH YOU." | "Whoever buys this business buys what transfers. A domain transfers. A page transfers. Thirty years of people knowing your name does not. That walks out the door with you." |
| 3 | 16.15 – 21.5 | Paper | "LEAVE SOMETHING / BEHIND." · offer · renewal · URL | "One hundred and ninety-nine dollars leaves something behind." |

The argument is a mechanism, not a valuation claim. It never says a business
with a website sells for more — that would be a statistic nobody here can
source. It says what transfers and what does not, which is checkable.

---

## What is deliberately absent from all six

No countdown, no scarcity, no statistic, no percentage, no named competitor, no
ranking promise, no delivery-time promise. The words "still", "finally" and
"even you" appear nowhere in the campaign's copy, narration, or on-screen type.
